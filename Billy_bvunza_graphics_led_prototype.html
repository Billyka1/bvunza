01 / The challenge

When the loudest narrative wins, public accountability loses.

Zimbabwe's digital public sphere is a space for debate and participation. It is also contested by partisan messaging, harassment, misleading claims and uneven access to credible information.

THE INFORMATION ENVIRONMENT

Many voices. Unequal reach. Competing claims.

Young people can encounter partisan narratives, coordinated messaging and hostile exchanges. Rural communities and people with limited access to diverse information sources may face additional barriers.

Partisan narrativesInformation gapsOnline harassmentDigital exclusion
THE BVUNZA INTERVENTION

Turn attention into an evidence trail.

A viral issue should not disappear when the timeline moves on.

BVUNZA monitors emerging concerns, verifies information, maps where issues occur and identifies the institutions with relevant responsibility. It then makes the findings shareable and tracks responses over time.

BVUNZA is non-partisan. It assesses claims individually, labels uncertainty and offers responsible institutions a fair opportunity to respond.

07 / Trust & safety

Credibility is not a slogan. It is a system.

Verification, privacy, fairness and contributor safety are built into the workflow—not added after publication.

◎

Evidence discipline

Check original sources, corroborate claims, label uncertainty and preserve verification notes.

◈

Source protection

Minimise personal data, restrict access and anonymise sensitive submissions.

↔

Right of reply

Give responsible institutions a fair opportunity to respond and publish attributable answers.

⌁

Transparent corrections

Correct errors visibly and keep a record of material changes to published issues.

◇

Fair responsibility mapping

Verify the actual mandate; distinguish service delivery from political oversight.

⊘

Do no harm

Assess risk before publication, moderate abuse and avoid exposing vulnerable contributors.

09 / Regional learning

A Zimbabwean method with regional learning potential.

Share reusable methods, not assumptions that neighbouring countries have identical institutions or political contexts.
